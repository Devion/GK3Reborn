# PBR material brief

The work package after the upscale. `texture-enhancement.md` covers producing a larger
base colour; this covers everything else a surface needs — normal, roughness, metalness,
height, occlusion — and how to make them on this machine rather than buy them.

Generated by `GK3Reborn.Tools pbr-plan` into `manifests/pbr-plan.json`, brought in by
`import-pbr`. Neither exists yet; this brief is what they are for.

## Why this is a separate pass, and why it comes second

A normal map is derived detail. Deriving it from a 64×64 diffuse gives 64×64 worth of
bumps, so the input has to be the enhanced base colour, not the original — which makes
this strictly downstream of `import-textures`. The 324 accepted pilot candidates are the
first eligible set.

It is also a different kind of judgement. An upscale is right when it looks like the
original at a larger size. A material is right when it behaves correctly as the light
moves, and a still cannot show that. The evidence for the two passes is not the same, and
running them together means neither gets looked at properly.

## What the renderer does with each channel today

Worth knowing before generating six thousand of anything. The mesh shader
(`Rendering/Vulkan/MeshShaders.cs`) shades Lambert diffuse and nothing else — there is no
specular term, no microfacet BRDF, and no tangent frame.

| Channel | Consumed today | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| Base colour | yes | — |
| Normal | no | a tangent frame, and a light with a direction |
| Roughness | no | a specular BRDF |
| Metalness | no | the same BRDF, plus a reflection source |
| Height | no | parallax or displacement; neither is planned before P10 |
| Occlusion | no | somewhere to multiply it that is not already the bake |

So the channels split cleanly. **Normal is the one that pays off first**: the RT and
dynamic tiers light from the authored rig (ADR 0007) and already vary `dot(N, L)` per
pixel, so a normal map changes the picture the day it is bound. Roughness and metalness
change nothing at all until the shading model grows a specular lobe, and generating them
before that is generating them blind — nobody can review what nobody can see.

Under the **compatibility tier the maps do nothing and should not be bound**. A baked
lightmap has already decided how every texel is lit; perturbing the normal underneath it
perturbs a term the shader does not use. Enhanced materials are an enhanced-tier feature,
in the same way the rig is.

### What has to change in the renderer

Four things, none of them large, all worth naming so they are not discovered one at a
time:

1. **A tangent frame.** `MeshVertex` is position, normal, UV, lightmap UV. Rather than
   adding a tangent to the vertex, build the TBN in the fragment shader from the
   screen-space derivatives of position and UV. `.ACT` clips rewrite vertex positions
   every frame (`ShapeMesh`), so a stored tangent would have to be rebuilt every frame
   with them; a derivative frame is correct for free, on deforming and rigid geometry
   alike.
2. **Two more textures in descriptor set 1.** It holds a diffuse and a lightmap per batch
   and never changes after load. Normal and a packed ORM make it four. Batches with no
   enhanced material bind the existing 1×1 white, which is how a partial set stays a
   perfectly good set.
3. **Linear upload.** Base colour is sRGB; normal, roughness, metalness, height and
   occlusion are **linear data, not colour**. Uploading a normal map through the sRGB path
   bends every normal towards flat, and reads as a weak, waxy surface rather than as the
   colour-space bug it is.
4. **Texture slots on `MaterialDefinition`.** It carries `Roughness`, `Metallic` and
   `NormalStrength` as scalars with no way to name a map. Adding `NormalTexture` and
   `OrmTexture`, and the matching fields on `MaterialPatch`, is what lets the edit layer
   correct a generated material the way it corrects a light.

## Constraints, which are mostly the upscale's constraints again

**Tiling must survive**, and it is easier to break here. A deterministic filter with
wrap-around convolution preserves it exactly. A diffusion model does not, unless it was
built to: noise rolling, circular padding in the VAE, or a tiling-aware node. An upscale
that breaks a seam shows a line; a normal map that breaks a seam shows a lit ridge that
moves with the camera, which is worse.

**Dimensions are fixed by the base colour**, not by the plan's tier target. A material's
maps and its base colour are sampled with one set of UVs.

**Alpha is a hole, not a surface.** 719 textures are magenta-keyed and the converted PNGs
carry real alpha. A generator reads the fringe around a chain link as geometry and
embosses it. Transparent texels must come back flat normal (128, 128, 255) and neutral
ORM, and no detail may bleed across the edge.

**The 280 flat colours get no maps at all.** They are a base-colour factor in a material.
Same rule, same reason.

**The originals have lighting painted into them.** 1999 diffuse textures carry highlights
and shadows an artist put there because there was no other way to have them. Two things
follow: a normal map derived from luminance turns painted shadow into geometry, and a base
colour multiplied by a modern rig darkens twice. **Delighting is part of this stage**, and
where the delight is not trusted the honest output is a weak normal and a flat roughness
rather than a confident wrong one.

**Text, heraldry and painted detail stay flat.** Embossing letters that were printed is one
of the most recognisable signs of an automatic material pass.

**Strength is a decision, recorded per material.** The upscale brief caps at 16× because
past that an upscaler invents rather than restores. The equivalent here is
`NormalStrength`: everything in a generated normal map is invented, so the number saying
how much of it to believe belongs in the manifest and in the edit layer, not in the
generator's defaults.

## Doing it locally

There is an RTX 5090 and a ComfyUI install at `D:\AI\ComfyUI` on this machine. Nothing
below needs a paid service, and the cost is wall-clock rather than money — which turns
"which method" back into a quality question, where it belongs.

### Lane A — deterministic, inside the importer

**DeepBump** (`HugoTini/DeepBump`) is a small ONNX model, **GPL-3.0**, doing
colour → normal, normal → height and normal → curvature, in 256-pixel tiles with an
overlap setting and a seamless mode for tiling input. It runs under
`Microsoft.ML.OnnxRuntime` with the CUDA or DirectML provider, which means it runs
**inside `GK3Reborn.Tools`** with no Python, no server and no second machine state: same
input, same model hash, same output, and the provenance record is three fields.

Its licence is the project's own licence, which is the cleanest position available and the
reason it is the default rather than the fallback.

Beside it, and worth building even if a model does the work, is the classical path:
high-pass the luminance for a height field, Sobel it with wrap-around for a normal, derive
occlusion from the height, and seed roughness from a keyword table over the surface name
(`STONE`, `GLASS`, `WOOD`, `CARPET`, `MTL`) plus local contrast. It is a hundred lines, it
is exactly reproducible, and it is the baseline a generative pass has to beat in a
side-by-side before its extra cost and its licence questions are worth accepting.

### Lane B — generative, through ComfyUI

For tier 0 hero surfaces, where a real SVBRDF decomposition beats a filter and somebody is
going to look at every one anyway.

| Option | Outputs | Licence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ubisoft CHORD** (`ubisoft/ComfyUI-Chord`, weights `Ubisoft/ubisoft-laforge-chord`) | base colour, normal, height, roughness, metalness, single-step | **Research-Only, copyleft** | The strongest thing on offer and **not usable here.** See below. |
| **StableMaterials** / **MatForger** (`gvecchio/*`) | base colour, normal, height, roughness, metalness; tileable by noise rolling; text- or image-conditioned | OpenRAIL | The candidate. Permits commercial use, carries use restrictions, is not OSI-approved — so it needs exactly the review `Plan/02` §1 already requires. |
| `comfyui_controlnet_aux` — DSINE, NormalBAE, Metric3D, Depth Anything V2 | scene normals and depth | mixed, per model | Already installed. Estimators for *photographs of scenes*, not for tiling surface detail: useful on a prop rendered from its geometry, weak on a wallpaper swatch. |
| Qwen-Image-Edit 2509 (+ 4-step Lightning LoRA) | an edited picture | Apache-2.0 | Already downloaded. Not a decomposition, but the most promising **delighter** on the disk — instructed to remove painted shadow and keep the albedo. |

**CHORD is the trap in that table.** It is Ubisoft's, its ComfyUI nodes are official, it
does in one step what the rest do in several — and its licence is Research-Only with a
copyleft clause, which is precisely the case `Plan/02` §1.3 says to refuse: it forbids the
distribution the project intends and attaches itself to derivatives. It may be worth
running once, locally, as a quality ceiling to measure lane A against. Nothing it produces
goes into `enhanced/`.

The installed node packs cover the plumbing: `comfyui_essentials`, `comfyui-kjnodes` and
`comfyui_layerstyle` for channel packing and tiling helpers; `comfyui-sam2` with the
Impact pack for segmenting a texture into material zones when one image holds painted wood
and metal at once; `seedvr2_videoupscaler` with `4x-UltraSharp` for the upscale lane that
already exists.

Note that `models/geometry_estimation` and `comfyui_controlnet_aux/ckpts` are both empty:
every model above is a first-run download, and pinning those downloads by hash is part of
making this reproducible rather than an afterthought.

### Driving it reproducibly

ComfyUI has an HTTP API — `POST /prompt` with an API-format graph, poll `/history`, fetch
from `/view`. The workflow JSON is pinned in the repository under `tools/pbr-workflows/`
with fixed seeds, and the tool records the graph hash, the model file hashes and the seed
per texture. That is `Plan/02` §1's "tool, model, prompt and settings" satisfied
mechanically rather than by somebody remembering what they clicked.

A generator that cannot be re-run to the same output cannot have its results attributed,
and a texture nobody can attribute cannot ship.

## Bringing candidates in

`import-pbr` is the counterpart to `import-textures`, and refuses on the same principle:
only things that make the game look **wrong**, never things that make it look different.

```bash
GK3Reborn.Tools import-pbr --workspace <ws> \
    --model enhanced/materials/deepbump-pilot --suffix _normal,_orm \
    --tool "DeepBump 256 via ONNX Runtime, overlap large, seamless"
```

Accepted maps land at `enhanced/materials/NAME_N.PNG` and `NAME_ORM.PNG`; the record goes
to `manifests/enhanced-materials.json`, which keeps **every** candidate including the
refused ones and why, for the same reason the texture manifest does.

Disqualifying checks:

- **dimensions**, which must match the base colour the maps accompany;
- **it must be a tangent-space normal map** — mean blue well above the others, mean red and
  green near neutral. A model that returned an object-space map, a greyscale height, or a
  colour image fails here, and each of those has happened to somebody;
- **tiling**, where the base colour tiles: the wrapped edge rows and columns must match
  within tolerance;
- **alpha regions**, which must be flat normal and neutral ORM;
- **flat-colour sources**, refused outright;
- **a degenerate roughness channel** — constant zero makes every wall a mirror, the single
  most visible way this can fail, and constant one means the generator gave up.

Everything else is recorded with a verdict of `draft`. As with textures, **passing every
check a machine can make is not the human review `Plan/02` §1 requires.**

## Judging it

Not in an image viewer. Three pieces of evidence, all of which the tools can produce:

1. **The same camera, maps on and maps off**, at `--rt high`. If the difference is not
   visible, the material is not worth its disk.
2. **A light that moves.** A still cannot distinguish a normal map from a painted
   highlight; a short orbit of the key light through the scene's rig can, and it is where
   an inverted green channel announces itself.
3. **Coverage on screen, not in the list** — the same measurement `texture-enhancement.md`
   uses, rendering the view with every material-bearing surface flat red. A tier of
   materials covering 3% of the frame is not a finished tier, and the base-colour pass has
   already demonstrated how easily that happens.

## Suggested order

1. The renderer's four changes, and one hand-authored normal map on one wall in R25,
   before any volume at all. Everything downstream is unreviewable until a map can be seen.
2. Lane A over the 324 textures that already have an enhanced base colour, normals only.
   Minutes of compute, and it establishes the review cost per texture.
3. A tier 0 comparison: twenty surfaces spanning stone, wood, fabric and metal, lane A
   against lane B, judged under the moving light. That decides whether lane B's licence
   questions are worth answering.
4. Roughness and metalness once the shading model has a specular term, and not before.
5. Height and occlusion last, or never — occlusion overlaps what the RT tier already
   computes, and parallax is not planned.
