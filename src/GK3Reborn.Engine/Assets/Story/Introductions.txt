# When Gabriel and Grace have been introduced to somebody.
#
# The port draws a label under the pointer and the original does not, so this is a
# question the original never had to answer. A scene names its people by their surnames —
# BUTHANE, BUCHELLI, WILKES — and a label that reads them back tells the player who
# everybody is the moment they walk into the square, before a word has been said. It is
# the same leak the second-floor doors had, where EMILIOS_DOOR and BUTHANES_DOOR
# introduced every suspect in the hotel to anybody who walked down the corridor.
#
# So until one of these holds, the label says what the player can actually see: "Woman"
# or "Man", taken from the character's own ShoeType in CHARACTERS.TXT rather than from a
# list kept here by hand.
#
# Every condition below is the game's own, copied out of the [LOGIC] section of an action
# file. Nothing is invented: where the shipped data writes MET_BUTHANE, that is what is
# written here, verbatim enough to search for. Which is also the limit of the list — a
# character the data never asks "have we met" about is not in it, and keeps their name.
# That is MONTREAUX, MACDOUGALL, MALLORY, MESMI, SIMONE, PRINCE_JAMES and
# MONSIEUR_BIGOUT; add a line when somebody finds the rule the game uses for one of them.
#
# GRACE and MOSELY are absent on purpose rather than for want of a rule. Gabriel arrives
# knowing both of them.
#
# One line per noun: the noun, a bar, and a Sheep expression evaluated against the story
# exactly as an action file's case is.

# RC1110A.NVC: MET_BUTHANE. She introduces herself while explaining the tour, so the
# tour-group topic is the introduction; there is no T_INTRODUCE for her.
BUTHANE        | GetTopicCount("BUTHANE","T_TOUR_GROUP") > 0

# CHU_1ALL.NVC: MET_ABBE.
ABBE           | GetTopicCount("ABBE","T_INTRODUCE") > 0

# CHU_ALL.NVC: MET_BUCHELLI, without the two clauses about what he is doing at the time.
BUCHELLI       | GetTopicCount("BUCHELLI","T_INTRODUCE") > 0

# LBY110A.NVC: INTRODUCED_EMILIO, without its clause about which room he is in.
EMILIO         | GetTopicCount("EMILIO","T_INTRODUCE") > 0

# MS2_ALL.NVC: MET_GIRARD. Grace meets her too, so the ego clause is dropped.
GIRARD         | GetTopicCount("GIRARD","T_INTRODUCE") > 0

# LBY110A.NVC: MET_JEAN. He is behind the desk and says his name when you walk up to it,
# which is why the rule is a noun and a verb rather than a topic.
JEAN           | GetNounVerbCount("FRONT_DESK","WALK") > 0

# MS3110A.NVC: MET_LHE. The two women are introduced together and the data treats them as
# one noun for it, so all three names turn over at once.
LADY_H_ESTELLE | GetTopicCount("LADY_H_ESTELLE","T_INTRODUCE") > 0
LADY_HOWARD    | GetTopicCount("LADY_H_ESTELLE","T_INTRODUCE") > 0
ESTELLE        | GetTopicCount("LADY_H_ESTELLE","T_INTRODUCE") > 0

# LHE_ALL.NVC: MET_LARRY. Not a topic at all — he is met by having been inside his study,
# which is what LHI is: ESTRINGS.TXT reads "loc_lhi = Larry's Study". LHE beside it is
# "Outside Larry's House", and standing on the doorstep is not meeting him. The study is
# the only room in the game he is ever in.
LARRY          | WasEgoEverInLocation("LHI")

# TR2_ALL.NVC: MET_MARCIE.
MARCIE         | GetTopicCount("MARCIE","T_INTRODUCE") > 0

# DIN112P.NVC and LER102P.NVC: MET_WILKES, and the second spelling of it, which allows
# for the introduction he gets in room 24 without a topic being raised.
WILKES         | GetTopicCount("WILKES","T_INTRODUCE") > 0 || GetFlag("IntroWilkes24")

# CD1_ALL.NVC: MET_TAXI_DRIVER. His noun is what he is rather than who he is, so it gives
# nothing away and he is not listed. Kept here as a note so nobody adds him.

# Who a save from later in the story cannot still be a stranger to.
#
# Every condition above is a question about what the player has said and done, and a save
# the original game wrote answers none of them: a .gk3 carries the timeblock, the room and
# the score, and not one topic count. So an imported game two days into the story drew
# "Woman" under Madeleine Buthane — the bug this file exists to prevent, arrived at through
# the machinery that exists to prevent the spoiler.
#
# What such a save does state is the point in the story it stands at, and that is enough.
# Each line below names a timeblock and everybody the story has put in front of the player
# by the time it starts; a save is credited with every line up to and including its own
# block. It is the same reasoning the score events are recovered by — a save standing at
# four in the afternoon has been through the morning — and it is generous in the direction
# the header above already argues for: a name shown a little early is a small spoiler, a
# name withheld from somebody the player has spent two days with is not.
#
# Everybody is met on day one, so a save from day two or three knows the whole list. The
# reader takes a later day as everyone rather than resting on that staying true.
#
# One line per timeblock: the block, a bar, and the nouns it introduces.

# Day 1, 10 AM. The tour in the hotel yard (RC1110A.SIF), the lobby, where Emilio is
# reading his paper and Jean is behind the desk (LBY110A.SIF), and the museum, where
# Girard stands in the office (MS2.SIF) and the two women in the hall (MS3110A.SIF).
110A | BUTHANE, EMILIO, JEAN, GIRARD, LADY_H_ESTELLE, LADY_HOWARD, ESTELLE

# Day 1, 12 PM. Wilkes in the dining room — DIN112P.NVC is the file MET_WILKES was copied
# from — and the church, where Buchelli and the Abbe are arguing (CHU112P.SIF).
112P | WILKES, BUCHELLI, ABBE

# Day 1, 2 PM. The moped opens the map, and the map opens the train station and Larry
# Chester's house: the game's own rule for leaving this block is satisfied by either of
# them, so the story counts both as reachable within it.
102P | MARCIE, LARRY
